How to Analyze a Rental Property

A step-by-step underwriting guide used by professional developers. No fluff, no guesswork — just the math that separates profitable rentals from money traps.

1

Determine Your Maximum Purchase Price

Before you fall in love with a property, calculate the maximum price that still makes financial sense. This is your anchor — every deal either clears this bar or it doesn't.

Maximum Price = (Monthly Rent × 12) ÷ Cap Rate − Renovation Costs

Example: Austin Duplex

Rent: $3,200/month total ($1,600 per unit)
Target cap rate: 8%
Renovation budget: $15,000

Max price = ($3,200 × 12) ÷ 0.08 − $15,000 = $465,000

If the property lists for more than $465K, either negotiate harder or walk away.

Common mistake: Starting with a listing price and working backward to justify the numbers. This is how deals go sideways. Always start with the math — not the property.
2

Calculate Monthly Cash Flow

Cash flow is everything. A property that looks good on paper but bleeds cash monthly will drain your portfolio over time. Here's the real breakdown:

Monthly Cash Flow = Rental Income − (Mortgage + Property Tax + Insurance + Maintenance + Vacancy Reserve)
ExpenseAustin Example ($260K duplex)
Mortgage (P&I, 7.5%, 20% down)$1,490
Property Tax (2.1%/yr)$455
Insurance$200
Maintenance (8% of rent)$256
Vacancy Reserve (5% of rent)$160
Total Monthly Expenses$2,561

Cash Flow Result

Rental income: $3,200/month
Total expenses: $2,561/month
Net cash flow: +$639/month ✅

The threshold: I require a minimum of $300/month positive cash flow before considering any rental property. Below that, the risk/reward doesn't justify tying up capital in illiquid real estate.
3

Calculate Key Ratios

Cash flow alone isn't enough. You need these three ratios to understand the full picture:

Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)

Cap Rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Purchase Price

Measures the property's return assuming all-cash purchase. Target: ≥ 8% for value-add, ≥ 6% for stabilized.

DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)

DSCR = Net Operating Income ÷ Annual Debt Service

Lenders require minimum 1.25x for investment properties. Below 1.0x means the property doesn't cover its own mortgage.

Cash-on-Cash Return

Cash-on-Cash = Annual Pre-Tax Cash Flow ÷ Total Cash Invested

Measures return on actual cash out of pocket (down payment + closing costs + renovations). Target: ≥ 10%.

Austin Duplex — Ratio Analysis

NOI: ($3,200 × 12) − ($455 × 12 + $200 × 12 + $256 × 12 + $160 × 12) = $38,520
Cap Rate: $38,520 ÷ $260,000 = 14.8% ✅
DSCR: $38,520 ÷ ($1,490 × 12) = 2.15x ✅
Cash-on-Cash: ($639 × 12) ÷ ($52,000 + $8,000 closing) = 12.7% ✅

4

Run the Stress Test

Every deal should survive worst-case scenarios. If it doesn't, you're overleveraged on optimism.

  • Vacancy spike: What if vacancy goes from 5% to 10%? Can you still cash-flow?
  • Interest rate increase: What if rates jump another 2% on refinance?
  • Renovation overrun: What if rehab costs run 20% over budget (they always do)?
  • Market decline: What if values drop 15%? Are you underwater?
  • Major expense: HVAC replacement ($8K), roof ($12K), foundation repair ($25K+)
Operator reality check: In 20 years of development, I've never seen a deal go exactly to plan. The ones that worked had margins built in for the things that went wrong. The ones that failed didn't.
5

Due Diligence — Don't Skip These

  • Structural inspection by licensed inspector (not the seller's contractor)
  • Title search for liens, encumbrances, ownership disputes
  • Rental comparables: 3+ recently leased units within 0.5 miles confirming rent assumptions
  • Sales comparables: 3+ comparable sales within 0.5 miles, sold in last 90 days
  • Permit history check — open permits or unpermitted work can cost thousands
  • Zoning verification — current use must be legal and conforming
  • Insurance quotes from 2+ carriers before making an offer
