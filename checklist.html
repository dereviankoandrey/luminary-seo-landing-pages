Income Analysis

0/6
Current market rent per unit verified (3+ comparable rentals analyzed)
Actual rents compared to market rent (if tenant-occupied)
Vacancy rate applied (5–10% depending on market stability)
Other income sources documented (laundry, parking, storage fees)
Lease terms reviewed for below-market or short-term leases
Rent growth assumptions documented and conservative (2–3% annually)

Expense Analysis

0/7
Property taxes verified (actual + projected after purchase)
Insurance quotes obtained for property type and location
Property management fee assumed (8–12% if self-managing)
Maintenance reserve set at 5–8% of gross rent
CAPEX fund established (roof, HVAC, water heater replacement cycles)
Utilities responsibility clearly assigned (owner vs tenant)
HOA fees, legal, and administrative costs included
🏦

Financing & Debt Service

0/6
Purchase price and comparable sales (comps) analyzed — 3 minimum
Down payment amount confirmed (20–25% for investment properties)
Interest rate and loan terms obtained from 2+ lenders
Monthly mortgage payment calculated including taxes & insurance (PITI)
DSCR calculated — target ≥ 1.25 for conventional lending
Closing costs estimated (2–5% of purchase price)
📈

Key Metrics & Returns

0/7
Cap rate calculated and compared to market average (target ≥ 5–8%)
Cash-on-cash return calculated on actual cash invested (target ≥ 8–12%)
Monthly and annual cash flow confirmed positive after all expenses
1% rule evaluated (monthly rent ≥ 1% of purchase price)
50% rule applied as sanity check (operating expenses ≈ 50% of gross rent)
Break-even ratio calculated (income ÷ total debt service)
Sensitivity analysis run — what if vacancy rises 3% or rent drops $100?
🔍

Risk Assessment & Due Diligence

0/7
Physical inspection completed — roof, foundation, HVAC, electrical, plumbing
Major repairs estimated (roof, HVAC, water heater, foundation)
Pest/termite inspection completed or confirmed not needed
Title search requested — liens, judgments, easements reviewed
Environmental hazards checked (flood zone, radon, asbestos if pre-1978)
Market absorption rate analyzed — how fast are similar properties renting/selling?
Exit strategy defined for each scenario (hold, sell, refinance)
✅

Final Decision Framework

0/5
All metrics meet minimum thresholds — no single point of failure identified
Deal analyzed with conservative assumptions (worst-case scenario)
Comparison made against 2–3 alternative deals in the same market
Personal capacity assessed — can you handle this property + current portfolio?
Final GO/NO-GO decision documented with rationale (write it down)

How to Use This Checklist Effectively

For rental properties: Focus heavily on Sections 2 (Income), 3 (Expenses), and 5 (Key Metrics). The cap rate, DSCR, and cash-on-cash return are your three most important numbers. If the deal doesn't cash flow in a stress-tested scenario, walk away.

For fix-and-flip deals: Pay extra attention to Section 6 (Risk Assessment) — repair estimates are where flippers lose money. Get 3 contractor bids for any repair over $2,000 and add a 15–20% contingency buffer on top of your lowest estimate.

For BRRRR deals: Your entire strategy hinges on Section 5 metrics. The "R" (refinance) only works if the appraised value supports it. Run your numbers assuming you'll get a refi at 70% LTV — not 100%. If the deal doesn't work at conservative LTV, it's not a BRRRR.

The golden rule: Never use best-case assumptions. Always run the worst-case scenario first. If a deal works when rent is $200 below market and vacancy is 15%, you've found something special. If it only works with optimistic numbers, pass.
