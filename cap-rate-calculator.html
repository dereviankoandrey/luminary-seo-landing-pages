📖 What Is a Cap Rate?

The capitalization rate (cap rate) is the ratio of Net Operating Income (NOI) to property value. It's one of the most fundamental metrics in real estate investing.

Cap Rate = NOI ÷ Property Value

Where NOI = Gross Income − Operating Expenses (NOT including mortgage payments)

🔍 Why Cap Rates Vary by Market

Metric TypeTypical RangeWhat It Means
Low cap rate (3–5%)Lower returnPremium markets, high appreciation potential, low risk
Mid cap rate (5–8%)Average returnBalanced markets with moderate growth and cash flow
High cap rate (8%+)Higher returnGrowing or distressed markets, higher risk, strong cash flow
⚠️ Cap Rate Trap: A high cap rate alone doesn't mean a good deal. It often signals higher risk — tenant turnover, neighborhood decline, or deferred maintenance. Always pair cap rate analysis with cash-on-cash return and DSCR calculations for the full picture. Our Deal Analyzer does all three simultaneously.

📈 How to Use Cap Rate in Your Analysis

  1. Screen deals quickly: If a property's cap rate is below your minimum threshold, skip it before doing deeper analysis.
  2. Compare properties: Cap rates let you compare apples-to-apples across different price points and markets.
  3. Value a property: If you know the NOI and the market cap rate, you can estimate fair value: Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate.
  4. Identify value-add opportunities: A low cap rate on a stabilized property might mean there's room to increase rents or reduce expenses.
