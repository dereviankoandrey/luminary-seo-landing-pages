What Is DSCR and Why Does It Matter?

The Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) measures whether a rental property generates enough income to cover its debt obligations. Lenders use it as the primary metric for loan qualification.

DSCR = Net Operating Income ÷ Total Annual Debt Service

How to read your DSCR:

  • DSCR ≥ 1.25 — ✅ Excellent. Most conventional lenders require this minimum.
  • DSCR = 1.00 – 1.24 — ⚠️ Acceptable but tight. Some portfolio or DSCR-specific lenders may approve.
  • DSCR < 1.00 — 🔴 Negative cash flow. The property doesn't cover its own debt. High risk for both you and the lender.

Key insight: A DSCR of 1.25 means your property generates $1.25 in income for every $1.00 of annual debt payment — giving you a 25% cash flow cushion against vacancies, repairs, or rate increases.

How to Improve Your DSCR

  • Increase rent: Even a 5% rent increase can push a marginal property into acceptable territory.
  • Reduce expenses: Shop insurance, self-manage if feasible, reduce vacancy through better marketing.
  • Larger down payment: Reduces loan amount → lower monthly payments → higher DSCR.
  • Longer loan term: Extending from 15 to 30 years lowers monthly P&I (though total interest rises).

DSCR vs Other Metrics

DSCR is distinct from the cash-on-cash return (which measures return on actual cash invested) and the cap rate (which ignores debt entirely). DSCR specifically answers: "Can this property pay for itself?" — making it the single most important number for rental investors seeking financing.
